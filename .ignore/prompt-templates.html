3D Prompt Templates

The meta-patterns of prompts.json — 63 prompts — turned into six fill-in templates with worked examples.

1. What the source file is

prompts.json holds a dict with two keys: title and prompts. prompts is a list of 63 objects. Each object has exactly two fields: title and prompt. There is no metadata and no code.

Who these prompts address

They are one-shot benchmark prompts for a coding model. Prompts 16 and 18 say it in plain words:

"The output should feel like a flagship benchmark scene for a frontier web agent."

Each prompt asks for one self-contained web page. 62 of 63 name Three.js. 33 forbid external assets. 48 demand procedural generation. The model receives no images and no models. It must write everything as code.

This single fact explains every pattern below. A grader looks at the first frame, moves the mouse, and judges. So each prompt blocks the shortcuts a model would otherwise take.

3. The shared spine

All six families use the same eight parts, in the same order. Only the length and the packaging change.

#PartJob

bNegative listFour named shortcuts. "This must not be A, B, C or D."
cGoal plus meansAn impossible density, then the technique that pays for it.
dTechnical boilerplateImport map. Procedural. No external assets.
eFirst frameWhat fills the screen at second zero. No menus.
fWorld and lifeNamed landmarks. Four or five independent motion systems.
gControlsPresets. Three compact controls. Photo mode. Reset.

The writer's core trick: paired opposites

Every prompt states the wow and the discipline that pays for it, in the same breath.

  • Density is paired with instancing.
  • Detail is paired with LOD.
  • Ambition is paired with a laptop frame rate.
  • A control is paired with a visible consequence.

That pairing stops the model from choosing between "pretty" and "runs". It must deliver both.

The degrade order

This is the sharpest device in the collection. It tells the grader what counts. Prompt 1:

"Quality selector degrades far window variation, traffic density and reflections before ever degrading island completeness or landmark silhouettes."

Without this clause, a model cuts the graded feature first to reach the frame rate.

Worked example

Billion-Piece Angkor — The Entire Temple City at Monsoon Dawn

Create a maximum-ambition Three.js environment of the ENTIRE Angkor temple city at monsoon dawn, alive, from the West Baray to the Bayon: nine hundred square kilometres of moated sandstone temples, flooded rice terraces, causeway processions and steaming jungle canopy, rendered as one continuous explorable object with the sun breaking through storm cloud behind the five towers of Angkor Wat. This must not be a single temple on a lawn, a ruin-and-vines mood piece, a fog-hidden partial model, or a dark stylized montage. It is the whole city, restored and inhabited, in one unbroken piece.

The goal is the visual impression of a billion individually considered pieces: hundreds of thousands of sandstone blocks with real courses and weathering, every bas-relief register on the outer gallery, a canopy of individually placed trees, the full moat and baray water system, causeways lined with naga balustrades and thousands of walking figures. Achieve this through brutal instancing discipline, procedural block and relief generation, merged temple shells and nested LOD, not through raw counts.

If you use Three.js, add an import map before the module script mapping "three" and "three/addons/" to the same pinned version, and import only via those names. Everything procedural; no external assets.

#### FIRST FRAME - THE WHOLE CITY IN ONE IMPOSSIBLE IMAGE
- Open on the complete temple city from high above the west, the sun climbing behind the five lotus towers of Angkor Wat: the vast rectangular moat catching the sky as a perfect mirror of pink and steel, the west causeway running dead straight to the gate, the Bayon's face-towers rising out of mist to the north, the Baray sheets of water flat and silver on both flanks.
- The city must read as dense and real: the temple silhouettes recognizable at a glance (Angkor Wat's five towers, the Bayon's crowded face-mountain, Ta Prohm swallowed in fig roots, Banteay Kdei, Phnom Bakheng on its hill), the grid of ancient roads etched by tree lines, the moats as hard geometry cut into soft jungle.
- The frame is full edge to edge: the Kulen hills blue in the north, the Tonle Sap floodplain hazing away south, storm cloud breaking overhead with shafts landing on individual temples.
- No menus, titles or reveals. Angkor is simply there, complete.

#### THE DESCENT IS THE SPECTACLE
- Design the entire experience around one continuous descent with no loading seams and no popping that breaks belief: from whole-city orbit, through temple-complex level where galleries and courtyards separate, down to causeway level where processions carry parasols and monks cross in file, and finally to relief level where individual carved figures on the gallery wall resolve with their weapons, headdresses and expressions.
- At the closest tier, the nearest gallery must genuinely reward inspection: the Churning of the Sea of Milk relief readable figure by figure, lichen and water stain in the sandstone courses, chipped naga teeth, offering candles, worn thresholds, moss in the joints, rain beading and running down the stone.
- Transitions between LOD tiers must be smoothly cross-faded or geo-morphed. The viewer should feel one coherent city, not tiles swapping.

#### ANGKOR, HONESTLY BUILT
- Respect the real macro-geography: Angkor Wat's west orientation and its exact moat proportion, the walled square of Angkor Thom with the Bayon at its dead centre, the five gates and their causeways of god-and-demon figures, the two Barays as enormous rectangles of water, Phnom Bakheng's isolated hill between them.
- Landmark set with true silhouettes and placements: Angkor Wat and its full three-gallery ascent; the Bayon and its two hundred faces; the Terrace of the Elephants; Baphuon and its raised walkway; Ta Prohm under strangler figs; Banteay Srei small and pink to the northeast; Preah Khan; the Srah Srang bathing pond.
- Everything else is procedurally generated but honest: laterite enclosure walls, corbelled galleries, false doors, lintel carving density falling off by importance, village stilt houses in the forest, rice terraces stepping down to the floodplain.
- Angkor Thom is a complete sub-world: its interior road grid, ponds, terraces, minor shrines, tree cover and the walled horizon on all four sides.

#### A CITY THAT IS VISIBLY ALIVE AT EVERY SCALE
- Water: the moats and Barays holding a mirror at dawn, rain squalls dragging visible curtains across them, ripple rings from fish, boats poling along the Siem Reap river, water spilling from temple spouts.
- Ground: processions along the causeways, monks in saffron files, market activity at the gates, oxcarts on the forest roads, workers on bamboo scaffolds repointing a gallery.
- Air and light: monsoon cloud shadow sweeping across the whole city, sunshafts landing and moving, mist lifting out of the canopy as the sun climbs, bats and swifts spiralling off the towers, smoke from cooking fires.
- Detail-tier life: candle flames in shrine niches, saffron cloth moving in wind, geckos on hot stone, rain running off every cornice and pooling in the courtyard flags.

#### LIGHT AND MATERIAL
- Monsoon dawn as default: low warm light raking the reliefs so every carved figure gains a shadow, wet sandstone reading darker and richer than dry, the moats as sheets of moving mirror, canopy foliage deep and saturated.
- Materials must vary by structure and age: warm grey-gold sandstone on the temples, rust-red laterite on the enclosure walls, green-black lichen in shade, pink stone at Banteay Srei, silver-grey fig root over Ta Prohm, gold leaf on shrine ornament.
- Sandstone carries believable variation: course lines, tool marks, water staining running down from every joint, no two blocks identical.

#### EXPLORATION AND CONTROLS
- Orbit, pan and zoom active immediately with beautifully tuned inertia across the whole descent range; one reset returns to the hero city view.
- Camera presets: full-city dawn hero; the west causeway walk toward the five towers; Bayon face-tower orbit at eye level; Ta Prohm under the fig roots; Phnom Bakheng summit looking back over the whole plain; a gallery relief macro pass along the Churning of the Sea of Milk.
- Compact controls only: weather (breaking dawn / heavy monsoon rain / clear blue afternoon), procession and activity density, and photo mode.
- Heavy monsoon is a required showpiece: the whole city under moving rain curtains, every roof running, courtyards sheeted with water reflecting the towers upside down, thunder light flashing across the canopy.

#### PERFORMANCE AS A FIRST-CLASS FEATURE
- This prompt lives or dies on LOD engineering: merged temple shells at far tier, instanced gallery and tower archetypes with per-instance variation at mid tier, bespoke carved blocks and relief panels streaming in near the camera, imposters for the far side of the plain.
- Processions, canopy foliage, rain and water surfaces are instanced systems with LOD behavior of their own.
- Quality selector degrades relief carving depth on distant galleries, canopy tree count and rain particle density before ever degrading city completeness, moat geometry or temple silhouettes.
- Smooth on a modern laptop, devicePixelRatio clamped to 2. The outcome: the largest religious structure on Earth, finally shown with the city that was built around it.

Worked example

Hyper-Fidelity Voxel Simulation Spec — Machu Picchu (Inca Prime)

#### OBJECTIVE

- Construct a "Hyper-Fidelity" Voxel Simulation of Machu Picchu at the height of the Inca empire, around 1500.
- The scene must be awe-inspiring, featuring the citadel in its inhabited state (thatched roofs intact, terraces planted and green), the Huayna Picchu peak rising behind it, and the Urubamba river gorge falling away on three sides.

---

#### VISUAL TARGET

- This is not a "ruin" simulator. This is Machu Picchu in its prime, roofed and worked.
- The resolution must be high enough to distinguish individual thatch bundles on a roof and the joint lines between the fitted stones of the Temple of the Sun.
- Daytime lighting must not feel flat or dull - maintain strong cinematic contrast (especially on the terrace stairs) and keep the cloud forest looking vibrant and "alive" across all times of day.

---

#### USER CONTROLS

- **Camera:** Cinematic Orbit with smooth damping, plus a walk mode along the terrace paths.
- **Lighting Control:** On-screen buttons for Dawn Mist / High Noon / Golden Hour / Starry Night + a Cycle button. (Keyboard shortcuts optional fallback, not required.)
- **Tour Mode:** On-screen button to toggle an auto-flyover following the Inca Trail in from the Sun Gate, down through the agricultural sector to the main plaza.
- **Default lighting preset:** Dawn Mist.

---

#### WORLD GENERATION LAYOUT (Low to High)

#### The Urubamba Gorge (Background and Base)

- **Topography:** Near-vertical granite walls dropping 450 metres to a river. Use noise functions for rock face detail. NOT a flat plane and NOT a gentle hill.
- **Color:** Wet grey-green granite, deepening to near-black in the shaded gorge.

#### The Citadel (Mid-Ground)

#### The Agricultural Terraces

- **Geometry:** Hundreds of stepped retaining walls following the true contour of the ridge. Use a stepped-voxel algorithm at high density so the curves read as smooth from a distance.
- **Placement:** Terrace runs must follow the slope continuously with no floating steps and no intersecting walls. Every terrace must connect to a staircase.
- **Material:** Dry-laid grey fieldstone (walls) + saturated green planted crop surfaces (maize, potato, quinoa in distinct blocks).

#### The Temple of the Sun and the Royal Sector

- Must be positioned accurately above the cave tomb, at the top of the fountain stair.
- High-detail voxel modeling for the curved wall, the trapezoidal windows, and the carved bedrock the temple sits on.
- Polished fitted ashlar with visibly tighter joints than the surrounding rough masonry.

#### The Residential Sector (Foreground)

- **The Houses:** Dense clusters of single-room stone buildings with steep thatched gables.
- **Thatch Effect:** Voxel thatch must use a layered warm-straw palette with wind animation and soft self-shadowing so it does not look dull or plastic.
- **The Water Channel:** A carved stone channel running down through sixteen fountains, with visible flowing water in every one.

---

#### ARCHITECTURAL DETAILS (The "Impressive" Factors)

- **Staircases:** Long carved-from-bedrock stairways linking every terrace level to the plazas, some cut from a single boulder.
- **The Intihuatana:** The carved ritual stone on its raised platform, casting a real shadow that changes with the lighting state.
- **Civilization:** Small clusters of llamas on the terraces, workers with carrying cloths on the stairs, and cooking smoke rising through the thatch to show scale.

---

#### ATMOSPHERE & LIGHTING

- **The "Inti" System:** The sun must be the dominant force, and it must break over the eastern ridge rather than appearing overhead.
- **Dawn Mist:** Cloud forest vapour filling the gorge and pouring over the saddle, the citadel emerging island-like above it, gold light on the peaks only (this should be the default).
- **High Noon:** Hard white light, short shadows, terrace walls reading as crisp horizontal bands, the granite almost glowing - avoid a flat look, use cinematic fill to keep form.
- **Golden Hour:** Long shadows striping the terraces, the thatch turning orange, Huayna Picchu's face in deep blue shade.
- **Night:** Dark blue sky with a dense Southern Hemisphere star field, hearth fires (orange point lights) flickering in the doorways.
- **Reflections:** If possible in the render budget, approximate the wet sheen on granite after the mist passes.

---

#### TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

- **Output:** Full implementation.
- **Engine:** Three.js (inlined via Import Map) or raw WebGL2.
- **Performance Strategy:**
  - InstancedMesh is Mandatory: You must instance the "Terrace Stone Voxel," the "Thatch Bundle Voxel," and the "Cloud Forest Tree Voxel." Do not draw 100,000 individual meshes.
  - Matrix Transformation: Use matrix manipulation to scale the gorge walls without killing memory.
  - Occlusion: Do not render the interiors of the residential houses unless the camera is inside one.
- **Color Palette:** Use a rich, saturated palette. Avoid "muddy" default colors.
  - Granite: Hex #8A9199 (Low specular, high detail).
  - Thatch: Hex #C8A34A (Warm, soft, no shine).
  - Terrace Crop: Hex #4E8B3C (Deep saturated green).
  - Mist: Hex #E8EEF2 (High scatter, low density).

---

#### IMPLEMENTATION GUARDRAILS

- **No External Assets:** All textures/models must be code-generated.
- **Solid Geology:** The terraces must mesh seamlessly with the natural ridge. No gaps, no floating walls, no terrace hanging in air.
- **Planning:** Ensure the citadel sits on the true saddle between two peaks; do not place it on a flat plateau. Flatten the terrain only where plazas are placed.
- **UI:** Keep the on-screen HUD minimal and functional (no extra commentary text).

---

#### FINAL INSTRUCTION

- Generate the full output.
- Focus heavily on the contrast between the sheer green void of the gorge and the ordered, human, sunlit geometry of the terraces.
- Make the scale feel "Vertiginous."

7. Procedure — write a new prompt

  1. Pick one subject and one exact moment. Name the light.
  2. Write the hook in one line. Example: "a forest wearing a million butterflies".
  3. Choose the family by target length. See the family table above.
  4. Write the intent sentence. Start with the verb "Create" or "Build".
  5. List four cheap answers a model would give. Use the negative-list word bank.
  6. Write the negative clause: "This must not be A, B, C, or D."
  7. State the impossible density. Name the unit: pieces, brushstrokes, wings, blocks.
  8. Name the technique that pays for it. Forbid the lazy alternative in the same sentence.
  9. Paste the import-map block. Choose the standard or the strict variant.
  10. Write FIRST FRAME. Describe only what fills the screen at second zero.
  11. Add the no-front-end clause. Use block 4.
  12. Name the hero mechanic in its own header. Name the failure it must avoid.
  13. List 6 to 10 real landmarks or features by name. Give true proportions.
  14. Write four or five independent motion systems. Give each its own bullet.
  15. State the default light state. Name 4 to 6 material classes.
  16. Write 4 to 7 camera presets. Separate them with semicolons.
  17. Write "Compact controls only:" then three controls and photo mode.
  18. Write the performance tiers: far, mid, near, imposter.
  19. Write the degrade order. Name what may drop and what may never drop.
  20. Close with "Smooth on a modern laptop, devicePixelRatio clamped to 2."
  21. Write the outcome sentence. Name the feeling, not the feature.
  22. Count the characters. Compare against the family range. Cut or expand.
Warning: do not skip step 19. Without a degrade order, a model cuts the graded feature first to reach the frame rate.

8. Pre-flight checklist

Check a finished prompt against these 14 items. The collection satisfies all of them.

  • The first sentence names the subject, the scale and the moment.
  • Four cheap shortcuts are named and forbidden.
  • The density target is stated with a unit.
  • The technique that delivers the density is named. The lazy one is forbidden.
  • The import-map block is present, word for word.
  • "Everything procedural; no external assets" is present.
  • The first frame is described. No menu, no title, no loading state.
  • At least six landmarks or features are named individually.
  • Four or five independent motion systems each have a bullet.
  • The palette is named. For family B, hex codes are given.
  • Four to seven camera presets are listed.
  • Controls number three plus photo mode. No more.
  • The degrade order names what drops and what never drops.
